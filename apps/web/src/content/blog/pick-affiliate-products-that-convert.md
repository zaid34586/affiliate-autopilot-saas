# How to Pick Affiliate Products That Actually Convert (A 5-Factor Scorecard)

Most affiliate income problems are not traffic problems — **they're offer problems.** You can run perfect content against a bad product and earn nothing. This scorecard gives you a repeatable way to evaluate any offer in under 15 minutes.

## The 5 factors

Score each factor from 1–5. An offer needs **20+** to earn promotion priority.

### 1. Commission structure (weight: high)

Raw percentage is not enough. Evaluate:

- **Net per sale.** $5 × 50% = $2.50 is worse than $80 × 20% = $16. Always do the math per sale.
- **Cookie duration.** 30 days is standard; 60+ is good; 24 hours is a red flag for any considered purchase.
- **Recurring vs one-time.** A $29/mo recurring commission at 30% ($8.70/mo) compounds. Ten referrals = $87/month that keeps paying.
- **Second-tier potential.** Some programs pay you when your referrals recruit others.

Score: 1 = under $5/sale and no recurring; 5 = $15+/sale with recurring payouts.

### 2. Product demand and gravity (weight: high)

Demand you can verify before promoting:

- **Search volume** for "product name + review" — if nobody searches for reviews, nobody's buying.
- **Existing competitor content.** Paradoxically, competition is a positive signal: it proves demand. Empty SERPs usually mean empty wallets.
- **Marketplace gravity** (ClickBank), **app store ranking**, or **review velocity** (how many reviews per month) as platform-specific proxies.

Score: 1 = unproven demand; 5 = obvious, quantified demand with active competitors.

### 3. Content fit (weight: medium)

This factor decides whether *you* can promote it naturally:

- Can you make 50+ pieces of content about it without repeating yourself?
- Does it fit your audience's existing pain? A productivity tool on a fitness page scores 1.
- Does the product demo well on video? Physical products with visible transformation score high; abstract B2B software scores low on Reels.

Score: 1 = forced fit; 5 = it's the obvious next thing your audience already asks about.

### 4. Refund rate and reputation (weight: medium)

A 60% refund rate voids a 50% commission. Check:

- Public refund policy — 30-day no-questions refunds are healthy; "no refunds ever" is a trap.
- Review sentiment on third-party sites (Trustpilot, Reddit, app stores). Read the 2-star reviews: are they about the product, or about expectations?
- Support responsiveness — bad support means churn, chargebacks, and angry comments under your content.

Score: 1 = refund rate unknown or reputation bad; 5 = transparent policy, strong reviews, responsive support.

### 5. Funnel quality (weight: medium)

The vendor's funnel is your silent partner:

- Does the sales page convert? If you send 100 clicks and nobody buys, the page is the problem — but you'll get blamed.
- Is there an upsell path? Bumps, order bumps, and upsells increase your effective EPC.
- Are swipe files, images, and demo videos provided? Vendors who equip affiliates are serious partners.

Score: 1 = bare landing page; 5 = proven funnel with assets and upsells.

## Scoring the scorecard

| Total | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 22–25 | Promote now — build a dedicated content series |
| 18–21 | Promote — test with 3–5 pieces first |
| 14–17 | Test small — don't build content around it yet |
| Under 14 | Pass — your traffic deserves better |

## Two offers beat ten

New affiliates spread themselves across ten programs and master none. The math favors depth:

- 3 offers × 20 pieces of content each = 60 pieces, deep expertise, compounding SEO and audience trust.
- 10 offers × 6 pieces each = 60 pieces, surface-level, no authority, no repeatable hook.

Run the scorecard, pick your top two or three, and give them 90 days of focused content before evaluating anything new.

## Re-run it quarterly

Gravity decays, refund rates change, vendors update funnels. An offer scoring 23 in January may score 16 in April. Quarterly rescoring takes 15 minutes per offer and protects the revenue you've already built.
