# 7 Hook Formulas That Make Affiliate Reels Stop the Scroll

You have 1.5 seconds. The first line of text on your Reel decides whether the rest of your content — and your affiliate link — ever gets seen. These seven formulas cover 90% of high-performing affiliate hooks.

## Why hooks decide everything

Reels distribution is a two-stage auction:

1. **Initial push** — shown to a small test audience. Watch-through and engagement decide the next batch.
2. **Compounding push** — each strong signal unlocks a wider audience.

The hook controls the single biggest signal: **2-second retention**. A weak hook loses 70% of viewers before your pitch starts.

## Formula 1: The negative callout

> "Stop doing [common mistake] — it's killing your [desired result]."

**Why it works:** loss aversion. People move faster to avoid a loss than to gain something. It also self-selects the audience — everyone doing the mistake *knows* they do it.

*Example:* "Stop posting Reels at noon — your reach is getting throttled."

## Formula 2: The result-first open

> "I [impressive result] in [timeframe]. Here's the exact system."

**Why it works:** proof before process. The result earns the right to teach. Keep the claim honest and specific — vague flexes get scrolled past as fake.

*Example:* "I made $1,240 in affiliate commissions last month with 4,000 followers. Here's the playbook."

## Formula 3: The curiosity gap

> "The [thing] that nobody talks about…"

**Why it works:** an open loop the brain wants closed. Use it sparingly — overuse reads as clickbait and erodes trust.

*Example:* "The affiliate metric nobody tracks — and it predicts revenue better than clicks."

## Formula 4: The comparison

> "[Option A] vs [Option B] — which one actually makes money?"

**Why it works:** viewers arriving at a decision take sides immediately. Comparison content also captures high-intent search and saves.

*Example:* "Amazon Associates vs digital courses — which pays more per 1,000 views?"

## Formula 5: The list promise

> "[N] [things] that [outcome]."

**Why it works:** bounded, scannable, and saveable. Odd numbers (5, 7) outperform round numbers slightly; specificity ("7 hooks" not "some hooks") outperforms vagueness.

*Example:* "5 affiliate products that pay $50+ per sale in 2026."

## Formula 6: The myth break

> "[Common belief] is wrong. Here's what actually [happens]."

**Why it works:** contrarian framing triggers mild indignation — the strongest passive emotion for comments. People comment to defend the myth, which boosts distribution.

*Example:* "Follower count doesn't matter for affiliate sales. Engagement depth does."

## Formula 7: The direct question

> "[Question your audience asks themselves daily]?"

**Why it works:** self-identification. The viewer answers internally, which creates a need for resolution — they watch on to find it.

*Example:* "Why do your Reels get views but no sales?"

## How to test hooks systematically

Do not intuit — experiment:

1. **Same body, different hook.** Publish the same content twice in a week with two hooks. Hold everything else constant.
2. **Measure 2-second retention** in insights, not likes. This is the hook's true score.
3. **Keep a hook journal.** Date, formula used, retention %. Patterns emerge within 20 posts.
4. **Retire hooks below your baseline** for three consecutive tests.

After ~30 tests you'll know which 2–3 formulas fit your voice and audience — and your affiliate content will start getting the distribution the offer deserves.

## The order of operations

Hook earns the view. First 3 seconds earn the watch. Content earns the trust. CTA earns the click. Commission earns the repeat. Most creators reverse this order — they pitch first and wonder why nobody watches. Hook first, always.
